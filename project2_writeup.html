From formulas to a Q1 turnover dashboard

A half-day virtual workshop that takes learners from looking up a single value to building a dashboard their manager can read in five seconds — built around a real HR turnover dataset, with a pre- and post-assessment designed to prove the transfer, not just the recall.

Role
Instructional designer & facilitator
Audience
PO specialists and operations team leaders
My background in the field
Built on an existing Excel and analytics background
Client
An international BPO group managing marketing and other services for several client brands
Timeline
TNA: December 2025. Workshop delivered: September 2026.
Format and tools
Virtual, instructor-led (MS Teams). Half day, about 3.5 hours including a break. Built on Gagné's Nine Events of Instruction.

Problem

Like the prioritization workshop, this one came out of the same company-wide training needs analysis. It flagged PO specialists and operations team leaders as spending time manually cross-referencing data across sheets and rebuilding the same reports by hand instead of working from formulas and pivot tables.

Whatever the audience's starting point, the real gap was consistent: people could read a spreadsheet, but couldn't pull a specific answer out of it quickly, and had no repeatable way to turn raw data into something a manager could act on without rebuilding it by hand every time the question changed.

Solution

A single half-day workshop built around two goals. Goal 1: build formulas to extract and analyze data accurately, using XLOOKUP to pull data across two linked sheets, and SUMIF/COUNTIF to calculate and count by condition. Goal 2: create visual reports to communicate insights clearly, using Pivot Tables, Conditional Formatting and charts — ending with learners building their own simple dashboard.

Every exercise ran on one real dataset: Q1 turnover records for 142 employees, split across two linked sheets so XLOOKUP had a genuine reason to exist, not a toy example. Each topic followed the same I Do → We Do → You Do rhythm: I build the formula live and narrate the logic, learners build the next one alongside me pausing at each argument, then they build one independently while I circulate.

Process

  1. 1

    Map the action

    Action mapping broke the goal into two measurable halves: extracting and analyzing data accurately, and communicating it visually. Each half was mapped to the specific Excel actions that would get learners there — XLOOKUP and SUMIF/COUNTIF for the first, Pivot Tables, Conditional Formatting and charts for the second — plus a practice activity for each: building a dashboard from a blended, multi-sheet dataset.

  2. 2

    Design the storyboard

    Built with Gagné's Nine Events of Instruction, topic by topic: gain attention with a real question the data can answer, state the objective, recall what learners already know (most already knew VLOOKUP, for instance, which made XLOOKUP a reframe rather than a cold start), demonstrate, guide practice, elicit independent performance, then give targeted feedback on the most common mistake before moving on.

    No topic opens with a slide. Every one of the six topics opens with the dataset already on screen and a question a supervisor or HR director would actually ask — "how much salary walked out the door through resignations this quarter" for SUMIF, for instance — so the formula always has a reason to exist before it's taught.

  3. 3

    Build assessments that test transfer

    The pre-assessment checks where learners start: can they pull a single value with a lookup formula, count and sum by a simple condition, build a basic pivot table. The post-assessment doesn't repeat those questions. It asks learners to handle a lookup that should fail gracefully, combine two conditions in one formula, write a plain-English conclusion a director could read, and decide what three things they'd check before trusting a new batch of data.

    The gap between the two is deliberate: the pre-assessment tests recall, the post-assessment tests judgment. Getting the right number is only half of the final two questions on the post-assessment — the other half is explaining why it matters or what you'd watch for, which is the part a formula alone can't teach.

  4. 4

    Facilitate and evaluate

    Delivered live over MS Teams, following the same I Do → We Do → You Do structure through all six topics, with volunteers screen-sharing their work for group feedback. Evaluated two ways: a post-training satisfaction survey, and a comparison of pre- and post-assessment scores to check actual skill gain, not just reaction.

Results and takeaways

Evaluated through a post-training satisfaction survey and a pre- vs post-assessment comparison, to check real skill gain rather than just reaction.

  • Building every exercise on one real, linked dataset instead of disconnected examples meant XLOOKUP had an actual job to do in the room, not just a syntax to memorize.
  • Writing the post-assessment to require a written conclusion, not just a correct formula, meant the assessment measured whether someone could hand off an answer a manager would trust — not just whether they could reproduce a formula.
  • Comparing pre- and post-assessment scores, rather than relying on satisfaction alone, was the point: it separates learners who enjoyed the session from learners who can actually do the work differently now.
